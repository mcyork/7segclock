7segclock

An NTP clock on four WS2812 seven-segment digits, driven by an ESP32-C3 Super Mini. Flash it from this page — no toolchain, no drivers.

Needs Chrome or Edge on a desktop. Safari and Firefox have no Web Serial, and this will not appear there.

After flashing

  1. The display shows AP and raises a network called mini7seg-clock. Password sevenseg.
  2. Join it; the setup page should open by itself. If not, browse to 192.168.4.1.
  3. Enter your wifi. It restarts, finds the time, and is then at mini7seg.local.

Wiring

Three wires from the C3, all on one edge of the board:

  1. 5V → panel H1 V
  2. G → panel H1 G
  3. GPIO4 → panel H1 DI

GPIO4 is not arbitrary. On the C3, GPIO2, 8 and 9 are strapping pins read at reset to pick the boot mode, and a WS2812 data line idles low — a strapping pin tied to one can drop the chip into download mode at power-up, which looks exactly like a dead board.

Updating later

Once it is on your network you never need this page again: go to mini7seg.local/update and upload firmware.bin.

That is firmware.bin, not firmware.factory.bin. The factory image carries the bootloader and partition table for a first flash over USB; sending it as an OTA payload writes a bootloader into an app slot.
